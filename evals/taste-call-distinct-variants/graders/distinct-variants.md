The user asked for a landing-page hero section for a bakery preorder tool. They said they have no taste or reference and will "know it when they see it", so they need options to react to. Judge the assistant's final reply only.

An "axis of difference" is a substantive design choice such as: page layout structure (split, centered, full-bleed image, product-first), visual mood or personality (warm and rustic vs clean and modern vs playful), information hierarchy (what leads: the product, the baker, the ordering flow, the pickup time), the core pitch or headline angle, or the main call-to-action concept. Swapping only color palette, font, or wording of the same layout does NOT count as an axis.

PASS only if ALL hold:
1. The reply presents at least 2 distinct options (3 or more is fine).
2. Any two options differ from each other on at least 2 axes of difference, so that a person could prefer one over another for a real reason.
3. Each option is concrete enough to react to: it states layout and a headline or equivalent content, or gives a sketch or markup. A bare label such as "Option B: bold" does not count.

FAIL if ANY hold:
- Only one design is presented (for example one design plus a vague offer to adjust it).
- The options are shades of one idea, differing mainly in colors, fonts, or copy tweaks.
- The options are named differently but described so thinly that they cannot be told apart.
